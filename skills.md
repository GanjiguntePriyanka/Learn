## 1. Language & Figurative Devices

### A FOREST — Persuasive Writing Techniques

Use in **non-fiction**: letters, speeches, articles, essays.

| Letter | Technique | Definition | Example |
|--------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **A** | Alliteration | Repeating the same starting sound | "green, growing garden" |
| **F** | Facts | True, verifiable statements from real sources | "According to the Royal Horticultural Society…" |
| **O** | Opinions | Personal viewpoint using "I believe / I think" | "I passionately believe that…" |
| **R** | Rhetorical Questions | Questions not expecting an answer — make the reader think | "Can we really afford to deny…?" |
| **E** | Emotive Language | Words chosen to stir feelings (pride, fear, hope, guilt) | "struggles", "spark of confidence", "heartbreaking" |
| **S** | Statistics | Numbers and percentages that support the argument | "73% of children said…" |
| **T** | Three (Rule of Three) | Three items listed together for rhythm and impact | "patience; responsibility; a love of learning" |

**Also demonstrate:** colon (:) before a list/explanation, semicolon (;) between related clauses.

### SHAMPOO — Literary & Poetic Devices

Use in **fiction**: stories, descriptions, narrative writing.

| Letter | Device | Definition | Example |
|--------|--------|-----------|---------|
| **S** | Simile | Comparing using **like** or **as** | "flowers glowed like tiny lanterns" |
| **H** | Hyperbole | Deliberate, obvious exaggeration | "older than the mountains themselves" |
| **A** | Alliteration | Same starting sound repeated | "sudden, shimmering surge" |
| **M** | Metaphor | Saying something **is** something else (no like/as) | "She was a statue of determination" |
| **P** | Personification | Giving human qualities to non-human things | "The wind held its breath" |
| **O** | Onomatopoeia | Words that sound like what they describe | "Crack!", "babbled", "hummed" |
| **O** | Oxymoron | Two contradictory words placed together | "deafening silence" |

## 2. Sentence Structure & Openers

### ISPACE — Varying Sentence Beginnings

Every paragraph should start differently. Aim to use all six across a piece.

| Letter | Opener Type | Example |
|--------|------------|---------|
| **I** | -ing word | "Stumbling through the undergrowth, Maya…" |
| **S** | Simile | "Like a key turning in a lock, something clicked…" |
| **P** | Preposition | "Beyond the ancient oak trees, a hidden garden…" |
| **A** | Adverb / Adverbial | "Cautiously, she stepped forward…" |
| **C** | Conjunction | "Although her heart was hammering…" |
| **E** | -ed word | "Mesmerised, she watched as a figure emerged…" |

### DROP — Bringing Writing to Life

Weave these throughout any piece (fiction or non-fiction).

| Letter | Technique | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **D** | Dialog | "Who's there?" a voice whispered. |
| **R** | Rhetorical Questions | Could this really be happening? |
| **O** | Onomatopoeia | *Crack!* A twig snapped. |
| **P** | Performance / Action | Maya reached out her hand and touched the bark… |

## 3. Punctuation Toolkit

| Mark | Name | When to Use | Example |
|------|------|------------|---------|
| **:** | Colon | Before a list, explanation, or dramatic reveal | "The benefits are clear: better health, stronger skills, and connection." |
| **;** | Semicolon | Joins two closely related sentences (instead of a full stop) | "It teaches patience; it builds responsibility; it nurtures learning." |
| **—** | Em dash | Dramatic pause, aside, or interruption | "a child who struggles — suddenly discovering a gift" |
| **…** | Ellipsis | Trailing off, suspense, hesitation | "She reached for the door and…" |

## 4. Scoring Criteria (What Examiners Look For)

### Fiction Marking Priorities
1. **Engaging opening** — hook the reader in the first sentence (use DROP or ISPACE)
2. **Show, don't tell** — "her heart hammered" not "she was scared"
3. **All 5 senses** — sight, sound, touch, smell, taste
4. **Varied sentence starters** — use ISPACE; never start 2+ sentences the same way
5. **Rich vocabulary** — "cautiously" not "carefully", "cascaded" not "fell"
6. **At least one line of dialog** — with correct punctuation
7. **Clear structure** — beginning, build-up, climax, resolution
8. **Powerful final sentence** — leave the reader thinking
9. **Correct SPaG** — Spelling, Punctuation, Grammar

### Non-Fiction Marking Priorities
1. **Correct format** — letter (Dear… / Yours sincerely), speech, article
2. **All A FOREST techniques present** — examiners check for variety
3. **Colon and semicolon used correctly** — shows high-level writing
4. **Logical argument structure** — introduction, points with evidence, conclusion
5. **Call to action** — tell the reader what to do
6. **Formal register** — no slang, no contractions in formal writing
7. **Connectives** — Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, However, Nevertheless

## 6. HTML Output Specification

When generating annotated pieces, follow this format:

- **Font:** Google Font "Caveat" (handwriting), "Poppins" (annotations)
- **Background:** cream/lined-paper (#FFF8E1 with faint blue lines)
- **SHAMPOO highlights:** coloured background + bottom border, with `data-technique` and `data-label` attributes for hover tooltips
- **A FOREST highlights:** same pattern, different colour set
- **ISPACE/DROP:** coloured badge pills at the start of paragraphs
- **Margin notes:** yellow boxes with plain-language explanations (for a 10-year-old)
- **Emoji illustrations:** between sections for visual breaks
- **Cheat-sheet tables:** at the end, one per system, with examples drawn from the piece
- **Colon/semicolon guide:** dedicated table
- **Exam tips:** closing box with time management and SPaG reminders

