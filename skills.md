## 1. Language & Figurative Devices

### A FOREST — Persuasive Writing Techniques

Use in **non-fiction**: letters, speeches, articles, essays.

| Letter | Technique | Definition | Example |
|--------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **A** | Alliteration | Repeating the same starting sound | "green, growing garden" |
| **F** | Facts | True, verifiable statements from real sources | "According to the Royal Horticultural Society…" |
| **O** | Opinions | Personal viewpoint using "I believe / I think" | "I passionately believe that…" |
| **R** | Rhetorical Questions | Questions not expecting an answer — make the reader think | "Can we really afford to deny…?" |
| **E** | Emotive Language | Words chosen to stir feelings (pride, fear, hope, guilt) | "struggles", "spark of confidence", "heartbreaking" |
| **S** | Statistics | Numbers and percentages that support the argument | "73% of children said…" |
| **T** | Three (Rule of Three) | Three items listed together for rhythm and impact | "patience; responsibility; a love of learning" |

**Also demonstrate:** colon (:) before a list/explanation, semicolon (;) between related clauses.

### SHAMPOO — Literary & Poetic Devices

Use in **fiction**: stories, descriptions, narrative writing.

| Letter | Device | Definition | Example |
|--------|--------|-----------|---------|
| **S** | Simile | Comparing using **like** or **as** | "flowers glowed like tiny lanterns" |
| **H** | Hyperbole | Deliberate, obvious exaggeration | "older than the mountains themselves" |
| **A** | Alliteration | Same starting sound repeated | "sudden, shimmering surge" |
| **M** | Metaphor | Saying something **is** something else (no like/as) | "She was a statue of determination" |
| **P** | Personification | Giving human qualities to non-human things | "The wind held its breath" |
| **O** | Onomatopoeia | Words that sound like what they describe | "Crack!", "babbled", "hummed" |
| **O** | Oxymoron | Two contradictory words placed together | "deafening silence" |

## 2. Sentence Structure & Openers

### ISPACE — Varying Sentence Beginnings

Every paragraph should start differently. Aim to use all six across a piece.

| Letter | Opener Type | Example |
|--------|------------|---------|
| **I** | -ing word | "Stumbling through the undergrowth, Maya…" |
| **S** | Simile | "Like a key turning in a lock, something clicked…" |
| **P** | Preposition | "Beyond the ancient oak trees, a hidden garden…" |
| **A** | Adverb / Adverbial | "Cautiously, she stepped forward…" |
| **C** | Conjunction | "Although her heart was hammering…" |
| **E** | -ed word | "Mesmerised, she watched as a figure emerged…" |

### DROP — Bringing Writing to Life

Weave these throughout any piece (fiction or non-fiction).

| Letter | Technique | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **D** | Dialog | "Who's there?" a voice whispered. |
| **R** | Rhetorical Questions | Could this really be happening? |
| **O** | Onomatopoeia | *Crack!* A twig snapped. |
| **P** | Performance / Action | Maya reached out her hand and touched the bark… |

## 3. Punctuation Toolkit

| Mark | Name | When to Use | Example |
|------|------|------------|---------|
| **:** | Colon | Before a list, explanation, or dramatic reveal | "The benefits are clear: better health, stronger skills, and connection." |
| **;** | Semicolon | Joins two closely related sentences (instead of a full stop) | "It teaches patience; it builds responsibility; it nurtures learning." |
| **—** | Em dash | Dramatic pause, aside, or interruption | "a child who struggles — suddenly discovering a gift" |
| **…** | Ellipsis | Trailing off, suspense, hesitation | "She reached for the door and…" |

## 4. Scoring Criteria (What Examiners Look For)

### Fiction Marking Priorities
1. **Engaging opening** — hook the reader in the first sentence (use DROP or ISPACE)
2. **Show, don't tell** — "her heart hammered" not "she was scared"
3. **All 5 senses** — sight, sound, touch, smell, taste
4. **Varied sentence starters** — use ISPACE; never start 2+ sentences the same way
5. **Rich vocabulary** — "cautiously" not "carefully", "cascaded" not "fell"
6. **At least one line of dialog** — with correct punctuation
7. **Clear structure** — beginning, build-up, climax, resolution
8. **Powerful final sentence** — leave the reader thinking
9. **Correct SPaG** — Spelling, Punctuation, Grammar

### Non-Fiction Marking Priorities
1. **Correct format** — letter (Dear… / Yours sincerely), speech, article
2. **All A FOREST techniques present** — examiners check for variety
3. **Colon and semicolon used correctly** — shows high-level writing
4. **Logical argument structure** — introduction, points with evidence, conclusion
5. **Call to action** — tell the reader what to do
6. **Formal register** — no slang, no contractions in formal writing
7. **Connectives** — Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, However, Nevertheless

## 5. Quality Checklist (Mandatory Pass/Fail)

**Every generated piece MUST pass ALL checks below before output.** If any check fails, revise the piece before presenting it.

### A. Sentence Count — 7 to 8 Sentences (Strict)

| Rule | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Minimum** | 7 sentences |
| **Maximum** | 8 sentences |
| **What counts** | A sentence ends with a full stop (.), question mark (?), or exclamation mark (!). Dialog counts as a sentence. |
| **What doesn't count** | Greetings ("Dear Mrs Thompson,") and sign-offs ("Yours sincerely") in letters. |
| **Why** | CSSE tests often have limited writing space. Examiners reward *density of quality* over length. Every sentence must earn its place. |

> **Self-check:** Count every full stop, question mark, and exclamation mark in the piece. The total must be 7 or 8.

### B. Vocabulary — Precise Verbs, Strong Adjectives & Sensory Details

| Check | Pass criteria | Fail example → Fix |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Precise verbs** | Every verb is specific and vivid — no generic "went", "got", "was", "had" | ❌ "She went to the garden" → ✅ "She stumbled into the garden" |
| **Strong adjectives** | At least 2 ambitious adjectives per piece (not "nice", "big", "good") | ❌ "a nice garden" → ✅ "an enchanting garden" |
| **Sensory details** | At least 3 of the 5 senses present (sight, sound, touch, smell, taste) | ❌ Only describes what things look like → ✅ Add "crunching" (sound), "frost-covered" (touch) |
| **Show, don't tell** | Emotions conveyed through *physical reactions and actions*, never stated directly | ❌ "She was scared" → ✅ "Her heart hammered against her ribs" |

> **Self-check:** Circle every verb — is it the *most specific* option? Highlight sensory words — are at least 3 senses covered? Search for "was", "felt", "seemed" — can any be replaced with action?

### C. Sentence Structure — Varied Lengths & Types

| Check | Pass criteria | Fail example → Fix |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Length variety** | Mix of short (under 8 words), medium (8–18 words), and long (18+ words) sentences. No two consecutive sentences of similar length. | ❌ All sentences 12–15 words → ✅ Follow a long sentence with a punchy short one |
| **Type variety** | At least 3 different sentence types used: simple, compound (and/but/so), complex (although/because/when), minor/fragment, question | ❌ All compound sentences with "and" → ✅ Mix in a subordinate clause and a dramatic fragment |
| **No repetitive openers** | No two consecutive sentences start with the same word or structure (ISPACE enforces this) | ❌ "She walked… She saw… She felt…" → ✅ Use ISPACE openers |
| **Rhythm & pacing** | Short, punchy sentences for tension/drama; longer flowing sentences for description | ❌ Same pace throughout → ✅ "Crack! A twig snapped." (short = tension) followed by a flowing descriptive sentence |

> **Self-check:** Write the word count of each sentence in the margin — is there variety? Underline the first word of every sentence — are they all different?

### D. Grammar, Punctuation & Spelling (SPaG)

| Check | Pass criteria |
|-------|--------------|
| **Tense consistency** | Pick past or present tense and stick to it — no accidental switching |
| **Subject-verb agreement** | "The trees were" not "The trees was" |
| **Comma after openers** | ISPACE openers MUST have a comma: "Cautiously**,** she stepped forward" |
| **Dialog punctuation** | Comma before closing speech marks when followed by a speaking verb: "Hello**,**" she said. Question/exclamation marks replace the comma: "Who's there**?**" she whispered. New speaker = new paragraph. |
| **Apostrophes** | Possession: "Maya's hand", "the tree's bark". Contraction: "didn't", "couldn't". It's = it is. Its = belonging to it. |
| **Homophones** | there/their/they're, to/too/two, where/were/we're, your/you're, its/it's |
| **Semicolons** | Both clauses must be complete sentences: ✅ "It teaches patience; it builds responsibility" ❌ "It teaches; patience and responsibility" |
| **Colons** | The part before the colon must be a complete sentence: ✅ "The benefits are clear: health, skills, connection" |
| **Spelling** | Zero tolerance — all ambitious vocabulary must be spelled correctly. If unsure, use a simpler word spelled right rather than a hard word spelled wrong. |

> **Self-check:** Read the piece aloud slowly. Does every comma, full stop, and speech mark feel right? Read it backwards sentence by sentence to catch spelling errors.

### E. Relevance & Tone

| Check | Pass criteria | Fail example → Fix |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|
| **Sentence count** | Exactly 7–8 sentences — no more, no less (see check A) | ❌ 12 sentences → ✅ Combine or cut to reach 7–8 |
| **Format match** | If the prompt asks for a letter → use Dear/Yours sincerely. If it asks for a story → use narrative prose. If it asks for a speech → use direct address ("Ladies and gentlemen…") | ❌ Writing a story when asked for a letter |
| **Tone match** | Fiction: imaginative, descriptive, emotional. Persuasion: formal, confident, logical. | ❌ Slang in a formal letter → ✅ "Furthermore" not "Plus" |
| **Prompt adherence** | Every sentence connects to the given topic/prompt. No irrelevant tangents. | ❌ Prompt asks about bravery but 3 sentences describe breakfast → ✅ Cut and refocus |
| **Completeness** | The piece feels finished — not cut off mid-thought. Has a clear beginning, development, and a strong ending within the 7–8 sentence limit. | ❌ Ends abruptly → ✅ Final sentence is deliberately powerful |

> **Self-check:** Re-read the prompt, then re-read your piece. Does every sentence serve the prompt? Count the sentences one final time.

### Quality Checklist Summary (tick before output)

```
□ A. Sentence count is exactly 7 or 8
□ B1. Every verb is precise and vivid (no "went", "got", "was")
□ B2. At least 2 ambitious adjectives
□ B3. At least 3 of 5 senses present
□ B4. All emotions shown through action, not told
□ C1. Mix of short, medium, and long sentences
□ C2. At least 3 different sentence types
□ C3. No two consecutive sentences start the same way
□ D1. Tense is consistent throughout
□ D2. All ISPACE openers have commas
□ D3. Dialog punctuation is correct
□ D4. Semicolons and colons used correctly
□ D5. All spelling is correct
□ E1. Format matches what the prompt asked for

□ E4. Piece has a clear beginning, development, and strong ending
```

## 7. HTML Output Specification

When generating annotated pieces, follow this format:

- **Layout:** Two-column grid — clean essay on the LEFT, technique annotation blobs on the RIGHT
- **Left column (essay):** Google Font "Caveat" (handwriting) on cream lined-paper background. Subtle coloured underlines on technique phrases, with small numbered circle markers linking to the right-side blobs
- **Right column (blobs):** Colourful rounded cards grouped by paragraph, each with a matching numbered circle, technique name, system badge (SHAMPOO/A FOREST/ISPACE/DROP), quoted phrase, and plain-language explanation for a 10-year-old
- **Font:** "Caveat" for essay text, "Poppins" for annotations and UI
- **Cheat-sheet tables:** at the end, one per system, with examples drawn from the piece
- **Colon/semicolon guide:** dedicated table
- **Quality checklist result:** After the cheat sheets, show the completed checklist from Section 5 with all boxes ticked and sentence count confirmed
- **Exam tips:** closing box with time management and SPaG reminders
