📖 Piece 1: Fiction — “The Secret of Willow Creek”

📝 Sample CSSE Prompt: Write a story about a time someone discovered something unexpected.
You have 30 minutes. Write between 250–350 words.
This story uses ALL of SHAMPOO, ISPACE and DROP. Watch for the coloured badges at the start of each paragraph — they tell you which sentence opener is being used!
🌳🌬️🏞️🌬️🌳

I – -ing P – Action Stumbling through the tangled undergrowth, Maya pushed aside the curtain of ivy that hid the entrance to Willow Creek. 👉 “Stumbling” is an -ing opener (ISPACE – I). The whole sentence is packed with action (DROP – P).

P – Preposition Beyond the ancient oak trees, a hidden garden stretched out before her — a forgotten paradise that time itself had overlooked. The flowers glowed like tiny lanterns in the fading light, their petals shimmering with colours she had never seen before. 👉 “Beyond” is a preposition opener (ISPACE – P). “Forgotten paradise” is a metaphor — the garden is not literally a paradise. “Like tiny lanterns” is a simile, comparing flowers to lanterns.

A – Adverb O – Onomatopoeia Cautiously, she stepped forward, her boots crunching on the frost-covered path. Crack! A twig snapped beneath her foot, and the silence shattered into a thousand pieces. 👉 “Cautiously” is an adverb opener (ISPACE – A). “Crunching” and “Crack!” are onomatopoeia — words that sound like what they describe. “Shattered into a thousand pieces” is hyperbole — obvious exaggeration.

🌫️🌳👁️🌳🌫️

D – Dialog “Who’s there?” a voice whispered from the shadows. The wind held its breath. The trees leaned closer, their branches reaching down like curious fingers. 👉 Dialog opener (DROP – D) hooks the reader. The wind “holding its breath” and trees “leaning closer” are personification — giving human qualities to nature.

C – Conjunction Although her heart was hammering against her ribs, Maya stood her ground. She was a statue of determination, rooted to the spot by a courage she did not know she possessed. 👉 “Although” is a conjunction opener (ISPACE – C). “Statue of determination” is a metaphor — she is not literally a statue, but the comparison shows how still and brave she was.

E – -ed word D – Dialog Mesmerised, she watched as a figure emerged from behind the great willow — an old woman with silver hair that cascaded down her shoulders like a waterfall. Her eyes were older than the mountains themselves, holding a wisdom that seemed to stretch back to the beginning of time.

“I have been waiting for you,” the woman said, her voice as soft as velvet. “The garden chose you.” 👉 “Mesmerised” is an -ed opener (ISPACE – E). “Like a waterfall” and “as soft as velvet” are similes. “Older than the mountains” is hyperbole. The dialog drives the plot forward.

🌟🧙‍♀️🌳🧙‍♀️🌟

R – Rhetorical Q Could this really be happening? Was she dreaming? A deafening silence fell over the garden. 👉 Rhetorical questions (DROP – R) — we do not expect an answer; they show Maya’s shock. “Deafening silence” is an oxymoron — two contradictory words placed together for dramatic effect.

S – Simile opener Like a key turning in a lock, something clicked inside Maya’s mind. She felt a sudden, shimmering surge of something magical. The leaves rustled and whispered; the brook babbled and burbled; and the whole garden hummed with an ancient, beautiful energy. 👉 “Like a key turning in a lock” is a simile opener (ISPACE – S). “Sudden, shimmering surge” is alliteration — repeating the ‘s’ sound. “Rustled”, “babbled”, “burbled” and “hummed” are onomatopoeia.

P – Action The colours were brighter; the air was sweeter; the world was more alive than she had ever known it to be. Maya reached out her hand, touched the bark of the great willow, and felt its heartbeat pulse beneath her fingers. She knew, with absolute certainty, that her life would never be the same again. 👉 The rule of three with semicolons — three parallel clauses separated by semicolons for rhythm and impact. The tree having a “heartbeat” is personification. The final sentence is a strong, confident ending — always finish with power!

Examiner Top Tips for Fiction:
✅ Use all 5 senses (sight, sound, touch, smell, taste)
✅ Show, don’t tell — say “her heart hammered” not “she was scared”
✅ Vary your sentence openers (use ISPACE!)
✅ Include at least one line of dialog
✅ End with a memorable final line

📣 Piece 2: Non-Fiction — “Why Every School Should Have a Garden”

📝 Sample CSSE Prompt: Write a letter to your headteacher persuading them to make a change at your school.
You have 30 minutes. Write between 250–350 words.
This persuasive letter uses ALL of A FOREST. Look for the coloured highlights — each one shows a persuasive technique in action! Hover to see the label.
🌱🏫🌻🏫🌱

Dear Mrs Thompson,

Have you ever wondered what it would be like if every child could grow their own food, breathe fresh air during school hours, and learn lessons that no textbook could ever teach? 👉 Opens with a rhetorical question (A FOREST – R) — a question you do not expect an answer to. It makes the reader think and immediately engages them.

I passionately believe that every school in Britain deserves a school garden. It is, without doubt, one of the most important changes we could make to transform the lives of young people. 👉 “I passionately believe” is a clear opinion (A FOREST – O). “Transform the lives of young people” is emotive language (A FOREST – E) — it stirs feelings and makes the reader care.

A green, growing garden would give students a space to learn, relax, and reconnect with nature. According to the Royal Horticultural Society, school gardens improve children’s understanding of science by allowing them to observe plant life cycles, soil ecosystems, and weather patterns first-hand. 👉 “Green, growing garden” repeats the ‘g’ sound — that is alliteration (A FOREST – A). The RHS reference is a fact (A FOREST – F) from a real organisation — facts make your argument trustworthy.

📊🌿❤️🌿📊

Astonishingly, 73% of children in a recent NHS survey said they felt calmer and happier after spending time outdoors during the school day. 👉 A statistic (A FOREST – S) — a number or percentage that supports your argument. Statistics make your writing sound researched and convincing.

A school garden teaches patience; it builds responsibility; it nurtures a love of learning that lasts a lifetime. 👉 The rule of three (A FOREST – T): three powerful points in a row, separated by semicolons. Semicolons (;) connect closely related ideas that could each be their own sentence. The rhythm of three makes your argument feel complete and powerful.

Imagine a child who struggles in the classroom — a child who feels lost among equations and essays — suddenly discovering that they have a gift for growing things. That moment of pride, that spark of confidence: it could change everything. 👉 Emotive language (A FOREST – E) — words like “struggles”, “lost”, “pride”, and “spark of confidence” make the reader feel something. The colon (:) introduces the dramatic conclusion — “it could change everything.”

Can we really afford to deny our children this opportunity? 👉 Another rhetorical question — the obvious answer is “No!” which makes the reader agree with you.

The benefits are clear: better mental health, stronger science skills, and a deeper connection to the world around us. 👉 A colon (:) introduces a list or explanation. Here it sets up another rule of three — three benefits listed together.

Surely, it is our solemn, sacred duty to provide the best possible education for every single child. I firmly believe that a school garden is not a luxury — it is a necessity. Let us plant the seeds of a brighter future, starting today. 👉 “Solemn, sacred” is alliteration again. “I firmly believe” is a strong opinion. The ending uses a clever metaphor — “plant the seeds of a brighter future” — linking the garden theme to the idea of hope. Always end with a powerful final line!

Yours sincerely,
[Your Name]

Examiner Top Tips for Non-Fiction / Persuasion:
✅ Use the correct format (Dear… / Yours sincerely)
✅ Hit ALL of A FOREST — examiners look for variety
✅ Use colons (:) to introduce lists or explanations
✅ Use semicolons (;) to join related ideas in a sophisticated way
✅ End with a call to action — tell the reader what to do next

🎯 Exam Day Reminders

⏰ Time Management: Spend 5 mins planning, 20 mins writing, 5 mins checking.

📝 Plan First: Jot down which SHAMPOO / A FOREST / ISPACE techniques you will use.

✅ Paragraphs: Use at least 4–5 paragraphs. Each one should have a clear purpose.

🏆 Opening Line: Make your first sentence grab the reader — use DROP or ISPACE!

🏁 Closing Line: End with a powerful, memorable final sentence.

🔍 Check Your SPaG: Spelling, Punctuation, and Grammar — read through once at the end!

✨ Vocabulary: Use ambitious words — “cautiously” not “carefully”, “cascaded” not “fell”.
